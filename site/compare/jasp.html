Compare / JASP and jamovi

Dioptra vs JASP and jamovi

JASP and jamovi are free, open-source statistics programs with a spreadsheet view, built on R. Dioptra is free and open source too, but aimed at lab experiments and figures: Prism-style tables, curve fitting and a graph editor for journal figures. JASP and jamovi cover more statistics, and JASP pairs most tests with a Bayesian version.

02 In short

Which to use

JASP or jamovi is the better choice when

  • you want Bayes factors next to every p value (JASP);
  • you need regression with many predictors, factor analysis, SEM, meta-analysis or mixed models;
  • you teach statistics in psychology or the social sciences, where both are common and well documented;
  • you want the R code behind each analysis (jamovi's syntax mode).

Dioptra is the better choice when

  • your data are lab experiments: dose groups, replicates, time courses, plate reads;
  • you fit dose-response or other nonlinear curves and report EC50s with confidence intervals;
  • you need the figure itself: sizes in millimetres, editable SVG and PDF, paired lines, SuperPlots, estimation plots;
  • you are moving from GraphPad Prism and want the same table kinds.

The three work well together. Dioptra exports any table as CSV or XLSX, which JASP and jamovi open.
