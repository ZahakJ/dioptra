Free and open source · Linux, Windows, macOS

From the instrument to the figure, without the spreadsheet.

Dioptra is a desktop program for scientific data. Paste raw numbers, shape them with formulas, run the test with its assumptions checked in plain view, and export the figure at the size the journal asks for. It does the work of GraphPad Prism and of the spreadsheet you opened before it, and keeps every step in one open file.

03 No spreadsheet in the loop

The errors happen before the statistics

The usual route goes through a spreadsheet. The plate reader exports a file, the file opens in Excel, someone tidies it, and the tidy version is pasted into the statistics program. Each step is a chance to change the data without noticing.

The spreadsheet reads the gene SEPT2 as the second of September and MARCH1 as a date in March. In 2016 about a fifth of papers that published gene lists from Excel had such errors; in 2021 the share was higher. Copy a block from the spreadsheet and the clipboard carries the rounded numbers it was displaying, not the ones it stored. Then the data are reshaped by hand to fit the next program's layout, and the baseline is subtracted in one column and forgotten in the next.

Dioptra takes the spreadsheet's jobs so there is nothing to round-trip:

  • Paste raw text. Tabs, commas or semicolons; decimal commas detected and reported. A value that could be read two ways, like 1,234, is kept as text and shown, not guessed.
  • Text stays text. A gene name is never a date, and <LOD is never a zero.
  • Read the file itself. XLSX, XLS and ODS are read directly, at the precision they were stored.
  • Shape it in place. Column formulas, baseline removal, normalisation, unit conversion, pivots and transposes, each stored as a recipe beside the data it came from.

04 Honest by default

Every assumption on the page

Equal variances: Brown–Forsythe, p = .03. The groups' spreads differ; Welch's test, used here, does not assume they are equal.

Welch's t test: the means differ by 3.2 units (95% CI 1.1 to 5.3), t(17.4) = 3.21, p = .005.

  • Checks run with the test. Normality, equal variances, sample size and outliers are tested and shown above the result, with what each one means for this analysis and the alternative when one fails.
  • Estimates before p values. Every sheet reports the effect with its confidence interval: mean differences, Hedges' g, odds ratios, hazard ratios, AUC.
  • Sentences you can paste. APA-style results and a methods sentence naming the test, its variant, the correction and the version.
  • Exact where affordable. Exact Mann–Whitney and Wilcoxon p values, Fisher's test beyond 2 × 2, and Welch, two-sided and Tukey–Kramer as the defaults.

05 Publication-ready

The figure on screen is the file you send

  • SVG and PDF that stay editable. Real text, named layers, no clipping masks; PDF embeds its fonts. PNG at any resolution.
  • Millimetres and points. 85 mm for a single column, 174 mm for a double; type and lines in points.
  • The same bytes everywhere. Fonts are bundled, so a figure made on Linux is identical on a Mac.
  • Colour-blind-safe palettes. Okabe–Ito by default; Tol, viridis and cividis besides. Points shown on every column graph.
  • Twenty-three families. Violin, raincloud, estimation, before–after, SuperPlot, Kaplan–Meier, volcano, forest and more, in three themes.

06 Correct, and shown to be

Tested against certified answers

The engine is checked against the NIST Statistical Reference Datasets, whose answers are certified to 15 digits, and against SciPy, statsmodels, lifelines and scikit-learn. Each check counts correct digits, and the whole record is published.

Where a certified answer cannot be reached in double precision because the inputs themselves cannot be stored exactly in binary (such as 1000000.1), the record says so and shows the comparison against exact arithmetic on the stored values. Read the correctness record.

07 Open

Free, open source, and your data stay on your machine

  • MIT licence. Free for any use, including commercial, with no account, no licence server and no expiry.
  • No network. No telemetry, no analytics, no update check. Dioptra works with the cable unplugged.
  • An open file. A .dioptra project is a zip of documented JSON: data, formulas, analysis and graph specifications, and the history of every command.
$ unzip -l assay.dioptra
